CONSISTENCY CHECK: does the portfolio have the beta its mu claims?
==============================================================================
  market volatility over the window : 14.92%

  Ledoit-Wolf long-only portfolio
      assigned beta (weighted average of the FF betas) : 1.093
      realised beta (regression on the market)         : 0.530  (t = 34.3, R2 = 0.61)

  The two disagree by a factor of two. mu says this portfolio carries
  1.09 units of market risk and should be paid for them; its own
  returns say it carries 0.53. The optimiser found stocks sitting in a
  high-beta 5x5 cell whose individual covariance with the market is
  low, and bought the discrepancy. That is an artefact of using coarse
  portfolio betas in mu and fine stock-level covariances in Sigma, and
  it is why the Ledoit-Wolf Sharpe ratio cannot be believed.

  Assigned minus realised beta: 0.17 for the main portfolio,
  0.56 for the Ledoit-Wolf one. That gap, not the volatility,
  is what separates them - both matrices predict their own portfolio's
  volatility perfectly well, because both were fitted on these same 750
  days. The difference is that the Ledoit-Wolf portfolio is *paid* for
  1.09 units of market risk by mu while carrying 0.53 of them. Its
  11.10% expected return is therefore not a return it can earn, and the
  1.11 Sharpe ratio is the size of the inconsistency rather than a
  reward. Under the single-index matrix that trade does not exist: mu
  and Sigma are built from one set of betas, so a stock can only be
  bought for its beta by also accepting the risk that beta implies.

  The residual 0.17 gap is real and worth naming in the video: the FF
  post-ranking betas come from monthly data over 2008-2025, while the
  realised beta is measured on daily data over 2023-2025. The same
  stock does not have the same beta in both.
