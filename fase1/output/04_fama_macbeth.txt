  The difference is tested month by month, so the market's own variation
  is removed from the standard error rather than left inside it.

================================================================================
WHERE DOES THE SURVIVORSHIP SHOW UP?
================================================================================

  1. In the level of returns, not in the slopes.
     our universe, equal-weighted :   1.282% per month
     the market (mktrf)           :   0.972% per month
     difference                   :   0.310% per month (t = 3.90), 3.8% per year
     Firms that were in the S&P 500 in December 2025 outperformed the
     market over a period they were selected for having survived.

  2. Not in the intercept of specification (6), which is misread easily.
     intercept as reported above  :   3.100% per month  (a firm with ln(ME) = 0, i.e. worth $1m)
     intercept with ln(ME) and ln(BE/ME) demeaned :   0.539% per month (t = 1.78)
     The slopes are unchanged by demeaning; only the intercept moves,
     because it is now evaluated at the average stock instead of at a
     point far outside the sample.
     slopes after demeaning: beta 0.726, ln(ME) -0.283, ln(BE/ME) -0.149

  3. In the size slope, which is the one to treat with suspicion:
     small S&P 500 firms of 2008 that are still in the index in 2025
     are survivors twice over, so -0.28% per month per log point of
     size is an upper bound on any real size premium.
